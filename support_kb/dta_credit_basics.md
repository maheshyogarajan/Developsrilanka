# Double Taxation Avoidance — foreign tax credit basics

Sri Lanka has Double Taxation Avoidance Agreements (DTAs) with ~40
countries, including the major source countries for foreign income
remittances: USA, UK, Australia, Canada, UAE, Singapore, Germany,
Netherlands, India, Malaysia.

How the credit works (general principle):
- If foreign tax was withheld at source on income that is also taxable
  in SL, the taxpayer may claim a credit against the SL tax liability
  for the foreign tax paid, up to the SL tax on that same income.
- The credit reduces the **SL tax payable**, not the gross income.
- Excess foreign tax (foreign tax paid > SL tax on that income) is
  generally NOT refundable in SL — you lose the excess.

Specific to the 15% foreign-income flat rate (PN/IT/2025/01):
- The 15% rate is final; foreign tax credit may be claimed against the
  15% liability if a DTA applies AND the foreign tax was on the same
  income remitted to SL.
- Evidence required: foreign tax certificate (withholding statement,
  P60/P45 equivalent, IRS 1099, ATO PAYG summary, etc.) plus the DTA
  article that applies.

Common pitfalls:
- Self-employment / freelance / gig income usually has NO foreign tax
  withheld (you'd pay it as estimated tax in the source country). If
  you didn't pay foreign tax, there's no credit — full SL tax applies.
- Salary income from a foreign employer with no SL PE typically has
  source-country withholding. Claim the credit with the foreign payroll
  summary.
- Investment income (interest, dividends) on retail accounts often has
  withholding via the foreign broker. The broker's annual statement is
  the evidence.
